## The bar

**The case-against must be genuinely adversarial. If it reads as a strawman it fails
the gate.** Write `[7]` as a CMO who *prefers the current site* and is good at their job —
someone whose objections are correct, not someone easy to rebut. If every objection in
the doc has a tidy answer, the doc is wrong and you have written marketing, not critique.

## Degraded mode, if WU-06 never unblocks

`[61]` (does analytics support search-takeover-after-engagement?) becomes
"cannot be answered without GA/Looker" — that is a legitimate finding, not a gap to hide.

## Method

Order matters — do `[55]` first, it is the no-go bar.

1. **`[55]` SEO implications — PRIORITY.** Riskiest assumption #4, the only one rated
   *low-to-medium* confidence, and the archive's own no-go bar. Quantify: which pages
   earn organic traffic, what happens to them under each concept variant, what is the
   crawlability story for a search-led homepage. If this comes back fatal, that outranks
   everything else in the project and must be stated at the top of the doc.
2. **`[7]` The case-against**, written as the smart CMO who prefers the status quo.
3. `[54]` buyer comprehension risk · `[56]` accessibility (see note above) ·
   `[57]` enterprise trust routing · `[58]` brand distinctiveness
4. `[59]` how much conventional nav must remain — answer with a number//proportion, not "some"
5. `[60]` compare radical vs balanced vs conservative variants. This directly sets
   WU-14's two frames; if the conservative variant wins on evidence, WU-14's frames change.
6. `[61]` does GA/Looker support search-takeover-after-engagement as a phased strategy
7. `[62]` which questions need named stakeholder input — Legal, Security, Sales Ops,
   Support, Brand, Product Marketing, Web Platform
8. `[63]` targeted stakeholder interviews *(Arijit — may block; degrade as above)*
9. **One validation task per riskiest assumption.** Each gets: assumption · current
   confidence · what would falsify it · who/what can test it.
